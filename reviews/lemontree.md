## Thesis

A clean, well-run hotel turnaround the engine cannot value positively. Every gate passes — zero pledge, conservative accruals (CFO > PAT, Sloan −0.06), Piotroski 7/9, 20% ETR, no event vetoes — and FY26 was operationally the best year in company history (73.5% occupancy, Rs 6,875 ARR, record revenue Rs 1,453cr / PAT Rs 288cr / cash profit Rs 443cr). But the frozen DCF (15% fallback margin, 13.6% capex intensity) values the equity at **minus** Rs 20.86 against Rs 2,004cr of debt: at Rs 112 the market is paying in full for the asset-light pivot (managed rooms, Fleur demerger, Warburg Pincus capital) before it has delevered the balance sheet. Quality is real; the price assumes perfection.

## What would change this

- **The binding constraint is the anchor, and it is assumption-driven:** the frozen model uses a 15% fallback EBIT margin vs Lemon Tree's actual 48% FY26 OPM, and a 13.6% capex-intensity dragged up by COVID-low-sales years. The negative FV says "at mid-cycle economics this debt load wipes out the equity" — a fair warning, but punitive for a hotel at 73.5% occupancy.
- **To INVEST AT TRIGGER / WATCH:** triggers are non-positive, so no price decline creates an entry under this model. The verdict flips only if (a) a future scoring FY shows materially lower debt (Fleur demerger + Warburg capital actually delevers the consolidated BS), or (b) the engine's schema ever carries actual OPM — until then the PASS stands as the model's honest output.
- **Watch instead for:** NCLT approval of the Fleur scheme, debt falling below ~Rs 1,500cr, and the 3.7%-by-FY28 expense-head promise printing in margins. Those are the fundamental developments that would make a re-run interesting.
